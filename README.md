# Plastic-Cube-Chair
# Virginia Tech WEE Summer Program – Award-Winning Foldable Baby Cube Chair

> **People's Choice Award Winner**
> Designed, engineered, and manufactured during the Virginia Tech WEE (Workshop for Engineering Education) Summer Program.

---

## Project Overview

During the Virginia Tech WEE Summer Program, my five-person engineering team was challenged to design a compact, affordable, and durable baby cube chair using additive manufacturing. The chair needed to safely support a child, include an integrated tray, be easy to transport, fold into a compact form for storage, and remain inexpensive to manufacture.

Our final design successfully met these objectives and was selected as the **People's Choice Award** winner during the final project presentations.

---

## Design Requirements

The chair was designed to satisfy several engineering constraints:

* Support a child weighing at least **26 pounds**
* Include an integrated baby tray
* Fold into a compact form for storage
* Be lightweight and easy to carry
* Be inexpensive to manufacture
* Maintain structural rigidity and stability
* Be manufactured using **PLA 3D-printed plastic**
* Fit within an approximately **7 × 7 × 7 inch** folded footprint (half-scale prototype)

---

## Engineering Challenge

This project required balancing several competing design goals. Increasing structural strength often increased material usage, while improving portability could reduce stiffness. Our team worked through multiple design iterations to optimize strength, manufacturability, usability, and cost while remaining within the project constraints.

---

## My Contributions

Our team consisted of five members, each contributing to different portions of the project.

During the concept phase, two teammates created CAD models for three prototype concepts. After evaluating those prototypes, I was responsible for engineering the complete CAD model for the final design by integrating the strongest features from each concept into one manufacturable assembly.

My primary contributions included:

* Designed the complete CAD assembly for the final chair.
* Combined successful design features from multiple prototype concepts into the final product.
* Refined dimensions and geometry to improve manufacturability and structural performance.
* Prepared production-ready CAD files for 3D printing.
* Operated and monitored the 3D printers, manufacturing the majority of the chair's components.
* Built and assembled much of the final prototype.
* Worked with teammates throughout design reviews to improve the final product before presentation.

---

## Prototype Performance

Although the project requirement was for the chair to safely support a **26-pound child**, our half-scale prototype significantly exceeded expectations.

### Required Load Capacity

* 26 lb minimum

### Tested Performance

* Successfully supported **56 pounds** without failure.
* Successfully supported **140 pounds** (my body weight) during informal testing without structural failure.

These results demonstrated a significant safety margin and validated the structural integrity of our design.

## Lessons Learned

This project reinforced the importance of iterative engineering design and collaboration. Developing the final chair required evaluating multiple prototype concepts, making trade-offs between strength, portability, manufacturability, and cost, and refining the design through repeated testing and feedback. It also provided valuable hands-on experience with CAD, additive manufacturing, prototype assembly, and communicating technical ideas during engineering presentations.

## Future Improvements

If additional time were available, I would explore:

* Finite Element Analysis (FEA) to further optimize the structure.
* Material reduction while maintaining strength.
* Improved hinge and folding mechanisms (had to use duct tape because we couldn't screw into the 3d print).
* Alternative materials with greater durability.
* Design for injection molding and large-scale manufacturing.
* Ergonomic improvements for both children and caregivers.
